Concept K

Tsuru — The Crane

The paper crane is Japan's most recognisable symbol — folded from a single sheet, gifted for good fortune, and associated with long journeys. Three strokes. The mark is as minimal as the origami process is deliberate.

Concept M

Postage Stamp

Every border crossed leaves a stamp. The perforated edge is immediately readable as philatelic — travel by mail, by journal, by passport. The 美人 sits inside like a country name on a first-day cover.

Concept N

ビジン — Katakana

Bijin in Japanese phonetic script: ビジン. No translation needed once you know it. The angular geometry of katakana pairs naturally with Josefin Sans. The name in both scripts, side by side — two alphabets, one identity.

Concept O

Noren — Split Curtain

A noren hangs in every Japanese doorway — you push through it to enter. Two panels, a gap in the middle, a threshold. The kanji hangs on each side. Every trip is stepping through a curtain into somewhere you've never been.
